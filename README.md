Pirates in the Caribbean during the Golden Age of Piracy (c. 1650-1740 CE)
had a unique way of organizing themselves. This module provides the baseline
for organizing the player characters and non-player characters of the pirate
crew in your [Foundry VTT](https://foundryvtt.com/) hosted tabletop roleplaying
campaign along the same lines.

* **Draw up your articles of agreement.** Designate which roles are open on
  your ship and how many shares each one gets. Pick and choose from provisions
  from historical articles or add your own.
* **Take a vote.** Provides scenes and macros to make it easy to take a vote on
  questions big and small, so you can play out the politics of life aboard a
  floating democracy of cut-throats and thieves.
* **Elect or appoint your officers.** The _usual_ arrangement was that the crew
  would elect the captain and the quartermaster, and the captain would appoint
  other officers as he saw fit. Feel free to stick to that standard model, or
  change it in your articles.
* **Set a watch.** The module loads the standard, historical watch system of
  the early 18th century as a default, but you can invent your own watch system
  for your world. Watch times are integrated with game time.
* **Designate watch teams.** Historically, pirate ships had a starboard watch
  and a larboard watch (“larboard” became “port” in the decades _after_ the
  Golden Age of Piracy), one headed by the quartermaster and the other headed
  by the sailing master, and they would switch off from one watch to the next.
  Keep that default or invent your own watch teams, and then assign the members
  of your crew to fill out each one.
* **Pay out treasure.** The quartermaster has access to a panel where they can
  pay out shares of treasure from the common stock, following the articles.
* **Track provisions.** By default, the module tracks rations of food, water,
  and rum, but you can change those to anything that’s important enough to
  track in your game.
* **Get better in your role.** At the end of each watch that a character
  completes, they gain that many hours of experience in their role (e.g.,
  the master gunner gains 4 hours of master gunner experience when she
  finishes the morning watch). System-specific modules can use this to
  apply new features or abilities when characters reach a required threshold
  of experience.
